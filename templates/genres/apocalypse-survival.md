# Apocalypse / Survival Genre Template

> **Core selling point**: the world's rules have collapsed and new ones are
> being written in real time. The appeal is resourcefulness under
> escalating pressure — the reader wants to see how the protagonist adapts
> faster than the situation gets worse.

## 1. The collapse's specific shape (define explicitly — "apocalypse" is
   not specific enough for the outline agent or reviewers to check against)

Pick and state clearly: what caused it (a specific, consistent mechanism —
not "everything" going wrong at once), what still functions normally
(nothing collapses uniformly — pick what survives and why, since that's
where interesting scarcity/conflict comes from), and what the new
threat(s) are (environmental, other survivors, a monster/infected
population, or a systemic one like resource collapse).

## 2. New-world rules (this becomes the project's core world-element note)

- **What changed**: the specific mechanism, stated precisely enough that
  the Continuity Reviewer can check chapters against it (e.g., not "zombies
  exist" but "infection spreads via X, incubates in Y hours, produces Z
  specific behavior").
- **What resources are scarce, and why**: scarcity should follow logically
  from the collapse's specific shape, not be arbitrary — this is what
  makes survival decisions feel like real trade-offs rather than
  author-imposed obstacles.
- **What power structures emerged**: who has authority now, and on what
  basis (control of a resource, prior skills becoming suddenly valuable,
  simple force) — this becomes the faction dynamics layer.

## 3. Faction dynamics

- **Survivor groups** with genuinely different survival philosophies (not
  just "good group" vs "bad group") — each group's approach should be a
  logical response to the same collapse, creating real ideological conflict.
- **The escalating threat**: whatever's actively dangerous should have its
  own internal logic/behavior pattern established as a world-element note,
  not escalate arbitrarily whenever the plot needs tension.

## 4. Classic payoff patterns

- **The skill nobody valued becomes essential**: a protagonist's
  pre-collapse knowledge or trait (unglamorous in the old world) becomes
  critical — works especially well when set up before the collapse if the
  outline includes a pre-collapse opening.
- **The group test**: a crisis forces the survivor group to reveal their
  real values under pressure — ties directly into faction-dynamics
  differentiation.
- **The line crossed**: protagonist does something they wouldn't have done
  before, forcing them (and the reader) to reckon with what survival costs
  — should trace to their Motivation Core's wound/fear, not just "things
  are desperate now."

## Entity types this genre typically needs story-bible notes for

The collapse mechanism itself (world-element, foundational — check every
chapter against it), each survivor faction and its philosophy, the
escalating threat's specific behavior/capability limits.
